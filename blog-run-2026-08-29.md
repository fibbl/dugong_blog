**Dispatch Nº 116 - "The expiry date your store can't see"**
`how-to-track-product-expiry-dates-on-shopify.html` · by Priya Singh · 2026-08-29 · 2,390 words (stated ~2,400) · 10 min

Topic: product expiry and batch/lot date tracking on Shopify, the carried-forward candidate
from the Aug 20-23 runs, finally run. Shopify inventory is one quantity per variant per
location: no expiry field, no batch or lot dimension, no report that mentions a date. The
post covers the community record (four dated threads, three with Shopify staff answers on
the record: October 18, 2023, merchant updating an expiry metafield per batch asks Flow for
an email ten days before the date, staff reply October 21 "this is not currently possible,
but Flow is working on better support for metafields"; June 17, 2024, a grocery seller asks
for 10/5/2-day notices plus automatic discounts, staff name the recipe and the wall the same
day, "you cannot yet use metafields with Get product data", then June 21 "It's not a Flow
limitation. The API we use, as well as all partners, doesn't support it", then July 5 "I
don't have a date yet, but I know it's being actively talked about"; January 9, 2025, a
merchant with 6,000 dated products, staff January 13: a recent API change made querying by
metafield value possible "but you cannot yet query by dates", suggested converting the date
metafield to single-line text, and "no straight-forward way to do that on a daily basis";
February 25, 2025, staff same thread "You cannot query by date metafields currently, so not
sure that use case is possible", followed by an app-vendor pitch; plus the lot-code thread
where a supplements seller was pointed to the app store); the single-metafield flaw (one
field, two batches, the fresher delivery overwrites the older date); the three costs
(write-off cliff, first-in-last-out picking shipping expired units, recalls with no
lot-to-order mapping); and the playbook (record every batch at receiving, daily date sweep,
60/30/10-day tiered alerts, markdown ladder that also reverses, listing pulled when the last
good batch ages out, lot stamped per order for recall lookups, weekly report), with the
honest-limits paragraph: nothing reads the label through shrink wrap, the receiving habit is
load-bearing, physical FEFO rotation stays with hands, and category policy stays with the
merchant.
